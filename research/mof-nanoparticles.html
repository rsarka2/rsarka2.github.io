Antibody-Targeted MOF Nanoparticles for Chemotherapy Delivery

Antibody-targeted metal-organic framework nanoparticles for delivering doxorubicin to triple-negative breast cancer cells.

  • April – December 2025
  • Teplensky Lab, Boston University

01

Key Metrics

90%doxorubicin encapsulation efficiency
78%antibody conjugation efficiency, verified using BCA protein assays

02

The Problem

Chemotherapy drugs like doxorubicin act on healthy tissue as well as tumors, and that off-target toxicity limits how they can be used.

Triple-negative breast cancer lacks the receptors targeted by several common breast cancer therapies, which keeps chemotherapy central to treating it and makes better targeting especially valuable.

03

Approach

I designed NU-1000 metal-organic framework nanoparticles functionalized with an anti-PD-L1 antibody. MOFs are porous, crystalline materials that can hold drug molecules inside their pores, and PD-L1 is a protein found on the surface of many tumor cells, so the antibody acts as a targeting handle.

The aim was to deliver doxorubicin preferentially to triple-negative breast cancer cells, reducing off-target toxicity and enhancing tumor cell killing.

04

Methods

  1. MOF synthesisSynthesized NU-1000 nanoparticles.
  2. Drug loadingEncapsulated doxorubicin in the MOF.
  3. Antibody conjugationFunctionalized the particles with anti-PD-L1 and verified conjugation using BCA protein assays.
  4. Cell studiesCell culture, confocal microscopy and flow cytometry.
