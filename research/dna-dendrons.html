Cleavable DNA Dendron Nanostructures for cGAS-STING Activation

Designing DNA-based nanostructures to reverse tumor immunosuppression by activating the cGAS-STING pathway.

  • June 2026 – Present
  • Teplensky Lab, Boston University
  • UROP-funded
Note

This work is ongoing and unpublished, so this page describes goals and methods only. No data or figures are shown.

01

Background

Many tumors are immunosuppressive: they create an environment that keeps the immune system from recognizing and attacking cancer cells.

One way to push back is the cGAS-STING pathway, an innate immune sensor that detects DNA in the cytoplasm of a cell. When it is switched on, cells release type I interferons such as IFN-β and chemokines such as CXCL10, which help activate dendritic cells and draw T cells into the tumor.

02

Design Rationale

Because cGAS is activated by DNA, DNA nanostructures are a natural platform for switching the pathway on. Dendrons are branched structures, and building them with disulfide linkages makes them cleavable: disulfide bonds can be broken by reducing agents such as glutathione.

I designed and synthesized cleavable DNA dendrons to address a delivery-related limitation in target activation that the lab identified through preliminary data.

03

Methods

  1. Design & synthesisDesigned and synthesized cleavable, disulfide-linked DNA dendron nanostructures.
  2. Cleavage protocolDeveloped a glutathione-based cleavage protocol, validated by gel electrophoresis and MALDI-TOF.
  3. Cell modelIsolated and differentiated bone-marrow-derived dendritic cells (BMDCs).
  4. Immune readoutsQuantified IFN-β and CXCL10 secretion by ELISA, and CD80, CD86 and MHC II expression by flow cytometry.

  • BMDC isolation & differentiation
  • ELISA
  • Flow cytometry
  • Gel electrophoresis
  • MALDI-TOF
  • HPLC

04

My Contributions

  • Designed and synthesized cleavable DNA dendron nanostructures to address a delivery-related limitation in target activation identified through preliminary lab data.
  • Engineered nanoscale DNA platforms to reverse tumor immunosuppression through cGAS-STING activation, quantifying cytokine secretion by ELISA and dendritic cell activation markers by flow cytometry.
  • Developed and optimized a glutathione-based cleavage protocol for disulfide-linked dendrons, validated by gel electrophoresis and MALDI-TOF, that is now used as a standard method in the lab.
