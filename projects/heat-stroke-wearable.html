Predictive Heat Stroke Wearable

A $30 wearable chest strap that predicts core body temperature from physiological and environmental sensor data.

  • 1st Place Overall · DS+X Hackathon
  • 1st Place · Health Track
  • October 2026
  • Team project

01

The Problem

Core body temperature is the signal that matters for heat illness, but it is hard to measure directly outside a clinical setting. The existing benchmark we compared against is the US Army's ECTemp model, which estimates core temperature rather than measuring it.

Our goal: an affordable wearable that predicts core body temperature more accurately than that benchmark.

02

My Role

I led the hardware: circuit design, wiring and assembly of the chest strap. I also built and delivered our final presentation, and contributed across the rest of the project.

03

Approach / Build

  1. Circuit designDesigned the sensing circuit for the chest strap.
  2. Wiring & assemblyWired and assembled the hardware into a wearable strap for about $30.
  3. PredictionCombined physiological and environmental sensor data to predict core body temperature.
  4. ValidationValidated on existing datasets containing 450 hours of data from 102 participants, benchmarked against ECTemp.

04

Results

22%lower prediction error than the US Army's ECTemp model
$30hardware cost of the wearable
102participants in the validation datasets (450 hours of data)

The project won 1st Place Overall out of 40 teams and 1st Place in the Health Track at the DS+X Hackathon.

05

What I'd Do Next

  • More comfortable housing: redesign the enclosure to be slimmer and lighter, with softer edges against the skin, so the strap can be worn for hours during training or work without irritation.
  • More training data: run additional datasets through the model to improve its accuracy and help it generalize across a wider range of people and conditions.
  • Additional sensors: add more physiological and environmental inputs to give the model a fuller picture of heat stress and further improve its predictions.
